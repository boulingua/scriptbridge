# Sound mappings

The executable table is in `extension/engine.js`. The source language and word context determine eligibility. This is an approximate, deliberately incomplete familiarisation tool, not transliteration or grapheme-to-phoneme conversion. No exhaustive pronunciation accuracy or educational efficacy is claimed.

- **Modern Greek:** consonants such as m→μ, n→ν, p→π, t→τ, k→κ, f→φ, l→λ. β corresponds to /v/, never /b/. English vowels and r are excluded. σ uses final ς where an eligible word-final s is changed. Reference: [Modern Greek Grammar, alphabet](https://www.greekgrammar.eu/pdffiles/greekalphabet.pdf).
- **Shared Cyrillic:** one introductory option with б, д, ф, к, л, м, н, п, р, с, т, з, а, о and ж, restricted further by source spelling. г, ґ, и, і and в are excluded because their use/sound varies. The introduction contrasts Russian г with Ukrainian г/ґ, Ukrainian/Belarusian і, Belarusian ў, Serbian/Macedonian ј/љ/њ and Bulgarian ъ. This is a shared subset, not every national alphabet. Existing Russian/Ukrainian saved settings migrate to this option. References: [Russian](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/russian.htm), [Ukrainian](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/ukrainian.htm), [Belarusian](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/belarusian.htm), [Bulgarian](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/bulgarian.htm), [Serbian](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/serbian.htm), [Macedonian](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/macedonian.htm).
- **Nordic:** Norwegian ø approximates German ö; Norwegian æ is used only for English a in a small explicit /æ/ word list. Swedish ä approximates French è/ê. These are selected letters from distinct languages, not a unified Scandinavian alphabet. References: [NTNU pronunciation](https://www.ntnu.edu/learnnow/1/pronunciation/calst), [Swedish vowel overview](https://blogs.transparent.com/swedish/pronunciation-of-the-swedish-a-a-and-o/).

Source spelling is only a heuristic: loanwords, names, stress, silent letters, dialect and consonant combinations can defeat it. Increasing the percentage increases exposure, not phonetic certainty. Add new languages only with an explicit table, contextual exclusions, a source reference, and positive and negative test cases. Arabic and Persian require bidirectional-text design; Japanese and Chinese are not single-letter alphabets and are not advertised as interchangeable character sets.

- **Polish:** French j→ż (/ʒ/), German z→c (/ts/), initial consonantal English y→j (/j/) and selected initial English w→ł (/w/). Do not map English j to ż. Reference: [Polish alphabet](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/polish.htm).
- **Czech:** French j→ž (/ʒ/), German z→c (/ts/) and initial consonantal English y→j (/j/). No unrelated single-letter substitutes are invented for č, š or ř. Reference: [Czech alphabet](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/czech.htm).

Icelandic æ normally represents /ai/, unlike the Nordic æ rule here; þ and ð need English th digraph handling and therefore remain introductory explanations, not replacement rules. Nordic language differences are stated in the popup and library. References: [Icelandic](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/icelandic.htm), [Danish](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/danish.htm), [Norwegian](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/norwegian.htm).

The learning-purpose disclaimer makes explicit that pronunciation, orthography, stress, dialect, grammar and context are only partially represented. Greek/Cyrillic expose more letters than the sparse Latin practice sets. Matching output length and 100% eligibility coverage are algorithmic properties, not claims of complete linguistic accuracy.
